Jun'ichirō Tanizaki's 1933 essay on aesthetics remains a key to understanding Japanese design.

## The Central Argument

Western aesthetics tends to favor brightness, clarity, and seeing everything at once. Japanese aesthetics, by contrast, values shadow, softness, and what remains just beyond view.

## A Line That Stays With Me

> We find beauty by creating shadows in places that might otherwise seem insignificant.

- Lacquerware is at its most beautiful in darkness.
- Noh theater is most moving in dim light.
- Sunlight filtered through paper sliding doors is essential to Japanese architecture.

## Lessons for Contemporary Design

Lighting without a dominant overhead fixture, warm color temperatures, and attention to material texture—all of these ideas can be traced back to this little book.

## After Reading

Reading it again in an age flooded with LEDs feels like drinking a glass of cold water.
