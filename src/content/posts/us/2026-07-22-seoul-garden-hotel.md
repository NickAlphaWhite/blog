Best Western Premier Seoul Garden Hotel is in Seoul's Mapo district, just steps from Mapo subway station. It opened in 1979 and was last renovated in 2014, so don't expect anything to feel particularly new.

My reason for staying here was simple: KAEC, the Korean AP testing center, is right nearby. If you're in Seoul to take an AP exam, it's hard to beat this location.

---

## Location: Hard to Beat

Mapo Station on Subway Line 5 is less than a two-minute walk away. Gongdeok Station is also within walking distance, connecting Lines 5 and 6 and the AREX airport railroad. You can take AREX straight from Incheon Airport to Gongdeok without transferring at Seoul Station. Airport buses 6015 and 6071 stop right outside the hotel, making the return trip particularly easy.

The alley behind the hotel is packed with neighborhood restaurants. Two specialties worth seeking out are gamjatang (pork-bone and potato stew) and gopchang (grilled beef intestines). These aren't tourist traps; they're the places where locals line up. A convenience store, Olive Young, and coffee shops are all within a minute's walk.

For visitors, the location is almost perfect. You're away from the overcommercialized crowds of Myeongdong but can get almost anywhere without much trouble.

---

## Rooms: Old, but Clean

The rooms are small, as you'd expect in many Korean hotels. A standard double room has barely enough floor space to open a suitcase. If two people are staying, you'll have to manage your belongings carefully. The bathroom is especially compact, with a shower over the tub. Water pressure is fine, but I kept bumping my elbows against the wall.

The décor is unmistakably dated. Carpets show wear, paint is chipped at the corners of some furniture, and the hallway ceilings are low enough to feel a little oppressive. But housekeeping is excellent. Rooms are cleaned thoroughly every day, and sheets and towels are changed regularly.

The mattress is firm—typical of Korean hotels. There's only one pillow per person, though you can ask the front desk for another.

Soundproofing is a weakness. You can hear conversations in the hallway clearly, and doors closing next door are audible. If you're a light sleeper, bring earplugs.

Some rooms have a balcony and bathtub. It's worth asking when you book, since the price difference may be small.

---

## Service

The front desk has Chinese-speaking staff, which is useful if you don't speak Korean. Check-in is at 3 p.m., and early check-in generally isn't allowed, as is common at Korean hotels. Checkout is at 11 a.m.

Luggage storage is free. Toothbrushes and toothpaste aren't provided at no charge—many Korean hotels have stopped offering them—but you can buy them from a vending machine in the lobby. The self-service laundry costs KRW 4,000 per load and takes cash only.

---

## Neighborhood

Mapo isn't a major tourist area, and that's part of the appeal. In the morning, you'll see office workers and students on their way to work or school. In the evening, the backstreet barbecue restaurants and soju bars fill up with locals. It's a more everyday side of Seoul than Myeongdong.

Hongdae is only three subway stops away, and you can reach Myeongdong, Gyeongbokgung Palace, or Gangnam in about half an hour.

---

## Verdict

For students taking AP exams in Seoul, Seoul Garden Hotel is an almost obvious choice. KAEC is right next door. There's no subway commute, no taxi, and no early-morning rush—just a short walk to the test center, which helps you stay calm before an exam. The rooms may be old, but they have everything you need. After your test, you can grab something to eat right downstairs.

The downsides are age, size, and poor soundproofing. If you can live with those three things, this is one of the best-value hotels in Mapo—especially for exam takers.
