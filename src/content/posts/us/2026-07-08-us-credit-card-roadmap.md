## My First Credit Card

### Discover it ($0)

![Discover It](https://www.uscreditcardguide.com/wp-content/uploads/discover_it_cash_back_FINAL-0a146207fcc24568a9667949f94a9d93-300x191.png "Discover It")

- 5% cash back in rotating quarterly categories, up to $1,500 in eligible spending per quarter; 1% in other categories
- No foreign transaction fee (FTF)

## Cards Two Through Five

Because of Chase's 5/24 rule, I need to prioritize Chase cards. Taking my preferences for oneworld and Marriott into account, here's my plan.

### Chase Freedom Flex (CFF) ($0)

![CFF](https://www.uscreditcardguide.com/wp-content/uploads/chase-freedom-flex-card.jpg)

This would be one of my dining cards. In addition to 5x Ultimate Rewards (UR) points in rotating quarterly categories, it earns 3x UR points on dining.

> ⚠️ Has a foreign transaction fee (FTF).

### Chase Freedom Unlimited (CFU) ($0)

![CFU](https://www.uscreditcardguide.com/wp-content/uploads/chase-freedom-unlimited-April-2021.png "CFU")

Along with 3x points on dining, it earns 1.5x UR points on other purchases. If you enjoy studying points and miles and maximizing their value, then with a Chase Sapphire Preferred (CSP) or Sapphire Reserve (CSR), that 1.5x on everyday purchases could be worth roughly 2.4% in my valuation.

> ⚠️ Has a foreign transaction fee (FTF).

### Chase Sapphire Preferred (CSP) ($95)

![CSP](https://www.uscreditcardguide.com/wp-content/uploads/csp-e1629138224670-300x191.png "CSP")

As I mentioned above, this card's value isn't just its earning rate. Once you have it, you can transfer Ultimate Rewards points to travel partners. I value those points at roughly 1.6 cents each. That means:

- CFF's 5x categories could be worth **about 8%**.
- Dining at 3x on CFF and CFU could be worth **about 4.8%**.
- Everyday spending at 1.5x on CFU could be worth **about 2.4%**.

The card also offers primary rental-car insurance.

> ✅ No foreign transaction fee (FTF).

### Chase Ritz-Carlton ($450)

![Chase Ritz-Carlton](https://www.uscreditcardguide.com/wp-content/uploads/marriott_ritz-card.png "Chase Ritz-Carlton")

You can no longer apply for this card directly, but it has been possible to product-change from Chase Marriott co-branded cards such as the Marriott Bonvoy Boundless.

**Key Benefits:**

1. Earn 6x points on purchases at Marriott hotels, 3x on dining, airline tickets, and car rentals, and 2x on other purchases.
2. Get up to $300 in statement credits for airline-related incidental expenses each calendar year, such as baggage fees, upgrades, and airport lounge passes—but not airfare itself. Unlike some other airline-credit cards, you need to contact customer service to request reimbursement.
3. Receive up to a $100 hotel-property credit when you pay cash for a stay of two nights or more at a Ritz-Carlton. Eligible charges include things like breakfast, spa treatments, and other on-property activities, not the room rate.
4. As a **Visa Infinite** card, it provides various Visa Infinite benefits, including Visa Infinite Hotel Collection, hotel privileges, and rental-car privileges.
5. Cardholders receive a separate Priority Pass Select (PPS) membership card, accepted at many lounges around the world, including in China—for example, selected airline first-class lounges in Beijing. It also allows guests without a clearly stated official limit on the number of companions.
6. Each calendar year, the card provides 15 elite night credits. These count toward Marriott elite status; they're **not** free-night certificates. A Marriott account can receive credits from only one personal co-branded card, even if you hold several. A Marriott business card may provide another 15 nights.
7. Up to $100 toward TSA PreCheck or Global Entry application fees.
8. No foreign transaction fee (FTF).
