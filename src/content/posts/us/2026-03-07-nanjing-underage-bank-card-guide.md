I'm not really a card collector, so I haven't opened that many bank accounts. But as a minor in Nanjing, I've dealt with quite a few banks. Here's a rundown of what I experienced, in case you're also a student who wants to open an account independently.

## The Short Answer (Start Here)

If you're under 18 and want an international debit card in Nanjing, go straight to China Merchants Bank. In my experience, it was the only bank that let someone 16 or older open an account independently with just a national ID card. Other banks either required a parent or guardian or wouldn't issue a card to minors at all.

Here's the bank-by-bank breakdown.

---

## Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC)

My parents opened my first account for me before I turned 16. The card works fine, and the app is decent.

I haven't tried opening an account independently here. I've heard that once your first card and personal information are on file, you may be able to open another one yourself, but I haven't verified that. Treat it as hearsay. If you have experience with ICBC, feel free to add it in the comments.

---

## Bank of China

**A guardian has to apply on your behalf.**

It's not just the initial account opening: a guardian must accompany you for all banking services, including getting a second card. That's why I still haven't applied for the Monet card. I'll wait until I'm 18. Repeatedly going back to the branch with a parent is just too much trouble.

It also doesn't help that staff at some branches seem less familiar with serving minors, which can mean long waits. On this front, Bank of China is definitely less convenient than China Merchants Bank.

---

## China Merchants Bank ⭐

**The gold standard. Nothing else comes close.**

At every China Merchants Bank branch I've visited, the tellers and relationship managers have been friendly, welcoming, and knowledgeable. Maybe that's why I've kept my Golden Sunflower account.

The most important point: **I didn't need a guardian with me.**

Right after I turned 16, I applied through the CMB app for an Arknights co-branded debit card. Then I brought my ID to a branch and activated it on my own. My parents weren't involved at any step. Later, I opened a Mastercard debit account the same way, with no issues at all.

If you're a minor in Nanjing and want to open a bank account independently, I'd go with CMB. I've visited branches in Xinjiekou, Gulou, and Hexi, and the experience has been consistently good.

The only thing to watch is the limit on non-counter transactions. CMB determines this internally. After I downgraded an older card and opened a new Mastercard, my daily limit became ¥50,000. That's plenty for everyday use, but large transactions require some planning.

---

## CITIC Bank

A guardian must come with you, and simply showing up together isn't enough. You'll also need a household registration booklet, birth certificate, or other documents proving the parent-child relationship.

The same rule applies to other banking services. It's inconvenient.

CITIC's Mastercard debit card has some nice features, including Apple Pay support at launch, but opening an account as a minor is a hassle. If your parent is willing to make the trip, bring the full set of documents and you'll probably have a good card afterward. If you want to handle everything yourself, as I did, CITIC isn't very realistic.

---

## Standard Chartered / HSBC

I haven't opened accounts with either bank, but when I asked, I was told that Standard Chartered requires a guardian to come along—and that the guardian must also be a Standard Chartered customer. HSBC gave a similar answer.

I'll leave those two until I turn 18. Foreign banks already have higher entry requirements, and the restrictions on minors make them even less appealing for now.

---

## Industrial Bank

I asked about this while accompanying my mom as she applied for a Global Life debit card. The branch said minors couldn't get that card and turned us down outright. Other debit cards also require a guardian.

The Global Life card is nice, but it isn't really an option for minors.

---

## Also Worth Mentioning: GO PAY

I came across this card by accident. The service was suspended for a while for various reasons, but it has since resumed. For minors, its distinctive advantage is that, as far as I know, it's the only foreign-issued card they can add to Apple Pay.

If you can't get a physical bank card yet, GO PAY might work as a stopgap. At least you'll have a card in your iPhone's Wallet.

---

## Final Thoughts

Think carefully about why you want a bank card. Don't open accounts just for the sake of it—especially if, like me, you're still a teenager figuring things out. Every card brings account management, transaction limits, possible annual fees, and other details you need to keep track of.

But if you really need one—for traveling abroad, shopping on international sites, or subscribing to overseas services—go ahead and try. The worst outcome is a teller telling you no. You can always try another bank.
