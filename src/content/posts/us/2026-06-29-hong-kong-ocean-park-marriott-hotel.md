I stayed at this hotel while working toward Marriott Platinum status through a status challenge. The reasons for choosing it were simple: it was close to Ocean Park, it belonged to Marriott, its category wasn't especially high, and it seemed like a reasonably good value among Marriott properties in Hong Kong. I stayed two nights and was upgraded to a suite. Overall, it was a good experience, but one problem was more noticeable than I'd expected.

---

## Location and Transportation

The hotel is in Wong Chuk Hang, right next to the entrance to Ocean Park. Wong Chuk Hang Station on the South Island Line is a five-minute walk away. It's two stops from Admiralty and also easy to reach from Central. From West Kowloon High Speed Rail Station, you can get here with one transfer in around half an hour.

There isn't much to do in Wong Chuk Hang itself. It's more of a transitional area, with Ocean Park on one side and residential buildings on the other. You can see the sea from across the hotel, with Lamma Island in the distance.

The main entrance to Ocean Park is genuinely only a two-minute walk away—just cross a pedestrian bridge. If Ocean Park is a big part of your itinerary, the location is hard to beat.

---

## Check-In and the Upgrade

I arrived around 3 p.m. There was no line at the front desk. When I handed over my ID, I casually mentioned that I was doing a Platinum Challenge. The receptionist checked the system, confirmed my status, and immediately assigned me a sea-view suite.

"We've upgraded you to a suite with an ocean view. The view is really nice," she said. That turned out to be true. Unfortunately, so did the humidity.

Check-in was quick. I had my key card in under five minutes, and the bell staff helped bring my luggage to the room. In the hallway, I noticed a faint mix of disinfectant and sea air—a first hint of the price you pay for staying near the water.

---

## The Suite

The room was on the third floor, and opening the door genuinely impressed me.

First, the space. In a city like Hong Kong, opening a hotel-room door and seeing a full living room instead of a bed is remarkable. There was a sofa, coffee table, desk, and an orchid. It was a different league from a standard room.

Then there were the floor-to-ceiling windows. Pulling back the curtains brought the whole South China Sea into view. The ocean stretched past both the living room and bedroom. Cargo ships occasionally passed in the distance, and beyond them you could make out the outline of Lamma Island. The night view was beautiful, too, with lights from the opposite shore casting long reflections on the water.

The bathroom was the real highlight. A large freestanding bathtub faced the sea-view windows. It wasn't one of those half-hearted arrangements where the tub is squeezed beside the shower. This one had the prime spot, right next to the glass. You could soak while looking out at the sea during the day or at the lights after dark. It was easily the best part of the suite.

Bath salts were provided, along with the usual Marriott thisworks amenities. Their scent was pleasantly fresh.

The bedroom was more conventional: a king-size bed with Marriott's typical soft-but-supportive bedding, which was very comfortable. The blackout curtains worked well, so I didn't have to wake up with the sun.

---

## That Musty Smell

Now for the problem.

The moment I entered, I noticed a damp smell. It wasn't overpowering, but it was obvious—the kind of mustiness you get when clothes won't dry during a rainy spell. It was confined to the room; the hallway and public spaces didn't have the problem. The bedroom was a little better, while the bathroom and the window-side area of the living room smelled stronger.

Turning on the air-conditioning helped, but the odor never disappeared entirely. I went out the next morning and found it had returned when I got back.

I don't necessarily blame the hotel's management. Hong Kong in summer, by the sea, on the third floor—those conditions practically guarantee humidity. Sea air, high moisture levels, and repeated changes between air-conditioned and outdoor temperatures create the perfect recipe for a musty smell. The room had a dehumidifier, but it could only ease the problem, not solve it.

If you're sensitive to moldy smells or have rhinitis, keep this in mind before booking. I got used to it by the second night, but that first impression wasn't pleasant.

---

## Food and Breakfast

This is a resort-style hotel, so Marriott Platinum members don't receive executive lounge access. Resort properties fall outside Marriott's usual lounge-access guarantee. I therefore chose restaurant breakfast as my welcome gift.

Breakfast was served at Marina Kitchen. Platinum members can select breakfast as their welcome amenity. The buffet was above average for Marriott properties in Hong Kong. Chinese choices included congee, dim sum, and stir-fried noodles; Western options included eggs, bacon, bread, and yogurt. The fruit station offered freshly cut mango and dragon fruit, both in good condition.

The most delightful surprise was the **Hong Kong-style cart-noodle station**. A cook prepared noodles to order, and you could choose from oil noodles, thick noodles, or rice vermicelli, then add classic toppings such as fish balls, daikon radish, pork skin, and fried tofu puffs. The broth was rich, the noodles springy, and the fish balls with radish were more satisfying than another helping of buffet bacon and eggs. I never expected the most memorable part of breakfast at a Marriott to taste like street food.

There was a bit of a wait at the made-to-order omelet station—roughly five to ten minutes during the morning rush. Coffee came from an automatic machine rather than being freshly hand-brewed, which lowered my overall impression a little.

There also aren't many restaurants within walking distance of the hotel. You can eat on-site or take the subway to Admiralty or Causeway Bay. Delivery options are limited compared with a city-center hotel.

---

## Pool and Facilities

From the outside, the outdoor pool area looks like one of the hotel's major selling points. It has separate adult, children's, and whirlpool pools, plus a small water slide. Unfortunately, the outdoor pools were closed for maintenance during my stay, so I couldn't swim. I peered through the fence: loungers, palm trees, and the sea beyond definitely created a vacation atmosphere. Hopefully I'll catch the pool open next time.

The gym is downstairs. Equipment is relatively new, with a good selection of cardio and strength machines. I spent half an hour on a treadmill. Better than nothing.

## What's Nearby?

- **Ocean Park:** Two-minute walk across the pedestrian bridge to the entrance
- **Wong Chuk Hang Station:** Five-minute walk
- **Aberdeen:** About ten minutes by taxi, with plenty of local seafood restaurants
- **Causeway Bay / Central:** Direct subway connections, roughly 15–20 minutes

---

## The Ups and Downs of a Platinum Challenge

During my Marriott Platinum Challenge, the benefits associated with Platinum status were active: suite upgrades, a welcome amenity, late checkout, and so on. This hotel honored those benefits properly and didn't seem to treat a challenge-period member any differently.

One thing to remember, though: as a resort-style hotel, it isn't required to provide executive lounge access under Marriott's terms. Even with Platinum status, there's no lounge to use here. Breakfast is available as a welcome-gift choice at Marina Kitchen rather than in a lounge.

The hotel's award category isn't especially high, and the points redemption rate can be reasonable. Using points during the off-season and paying a little extra in cash saved me quite a bit over booking the two nights entirely with cash. If you're pursuing Platinum status, this can be a good-value choice—as long as you're comfortable with the lack of a lounge and the humidity by the sea.

---

## Verdict

This hotel has very clear strengths and weaknesses.

**The good:** A sea-view suite with this much space and such a sweeping outlook is unusual in Hong Kong. The bathtub overlooking the water is a genuinely relaxing spot. The cart-noodle breakfast station was unexpectedly excellent. Suite upgrades for Platinum members were handled generously.

**The not-so-good:** A lingering musty smell from the coastal humidity, which the air conditioner and dehumidifier could only reduce. No lounge access for Platinum members because of the resort classification. The outdoor pool was closed for maintenance during my stay. And beyond Ocean Park, there's not much to explore on foot.

If you're looking for a Marriott near Ocean Park that treats Platinum guests well and offers an ocean view, this is a sensible choice. But if you can't stand damp rooms or musty smells, I'd suggest staying downtown.

**My rating: 4/5** (provided you're willing to live with some coastal humidity).
