Starting January 15, 2026, Apple Pay in mainland China officially supports Visa credit and debit cards issued by domestic banks. Eight banks are joining at launch.

It's been a full decade since Apple Pay entered China in 2016 with UnionPay-only support. Visa is the first international card network to join Apple Pay in mainland China.

## Banks Available at Launch

The following banks support Visa on Apple Pay as of today.

**Visa credit cards (eight banks):**

- Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC)
- Bank of China
- Agricultural Bank of China
- Bank of Communications
- China Merchants Bank
- China CITIC Bank
- Ping An Bank
- Industrial Bank

**Visa debit cards:**

- Only CITIC Bank Visa debit cards are supported at launch.

Banks expected to join later include Shanghai Pudong Development Bank, China Construction Bank, China Minsheng Bank, and China Everbright Bank. Support is expected to roll out over the coming months, eventually covering most major domestic banks.

## How to Add Your Card

**Add it directly through Wallet:**

Open the Wallet app on your iPhone → tap the plus sign in the upper-right corner → select "Debit or Credit Card" → choose your issuing bank → follow the prompts to verify through the bank's app → finish setup.

For this method, your iPhone region needs to be set to mainland China.

**Add it through your bank's app (recommended):**

Search for "Apple Pay" in your bank's app or open the card-management page. From there, look for an option that sends you directly to Wallet. This method isn't affected by your iPhone's region setting and tends to work more reliably.

Before you start, make sure iOS and your banking app are up to date, the card isn't locked, and international transactions are enabled. Some banks require a separate toggle in their apps.

## Where You Can Use It

For now, Visa on Apple Pay **only supports payments outside mainland China**. Domestic UnionPay point-of-sale terminals aren't covered.

Think convenience stores in Japan, subway systems in Europe, buses in Singapore, and restaurants in North America—any merchant that accepts Visa contactless payments. Double-click the side button, authenticate with Face ID, tap, and go.

One practical example is buying an international data plan through an eSIM platform such as RedteaGO. With Visa set up in Apple Pay, you can buy data before a trip or top up on the road without typing in your card number.

## Security

- Your actual card number isn't stored on your device or on Apple's servers.
- Each transaction uses a one-time dynamic security code through tokenization.
- If you lose your device, you can remove the card remotely using Find My.
- Payments require Face ID or Touch ID authentication.

## What About Mastercard?

Mastercard is still missing from Apple Pay in mainland China. Mastercard NUCC, its joint-venture clearing operation in China, began operating in May 2024 and has issued yuan-denominated Mastercard cards, but an Apple Pay launch has yet to happen. Rumor has it that one is coming soon.

For Mastercard holders—especially people with CITIC or China Merchants Bank Mastercard debit cards—Visa's full launch might inspire a little envy. On the bright side, Visa has shown that integration is technically and regulatorily feasible for an international card network. Mastercard may not be far behind.

## Bottom Line

After ten years, Visa cards issued in China can finally go into an iPhone's Wallet. Eight banks are available right away, covering nearly all the major credit-card issuers. This isn't a slow trickle of support; it's a substantial launch.

Debit-card support is limited to CITIC Bank for now, but that should improve as more banks join. If you have an eligible credit card, you can give it a try today.
