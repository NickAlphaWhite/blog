Tokyo Grand Hotel is in Shiba, Minato City, about a 15-minute walk from Tokyo Tower. It's not new and still has a distinctly Showa-era feel, but it's well maintained. My biggest takeaway from staying here: if you book a room facing Tokyo Tower, the view alone justifies the rate.

---

## Location

The hotel sits beside Shiba Park in Minato. Shibakoen Station on the Toei Mita Line is two minutes away on foot, and Hamamatsucho Station, served by the JR Yamanote Line and Tokyo Monorail, is about ten minutes away. From Haneda Airport, take the monorail to Hamamatsucho and walk over; the entire trip takes roughly half an hour. Getting to Narita requires a transfer around Tokyo Station or Ueno.

Tokyo Tower is clearly visible nearby. Walking through Shiba Park during the day and returning to watch the tower light up at night is a combination that's hard to find at this price point in Tokyo. The neighborhood mixes offices and homes, so it isn't particularly lively, but it is quiet. Convenience stores and a few restaurants are within walking distance. For shopping, you'll probably head to Ginza or Roppongi, about 10–15 minutes away by subway.

---

## The Room

The atmosphere is decidedly Showa: dark wood furniture, warm lighting, and heavy curtains. It's not the trendy, Instagram-ready style of newer hotels. Instead, it has an understated, old-fashioned solidity. The rooms are spacious for the price by Japanese standards. You can open two suitcases without a problem.

Housekeeping is excellent. Carpets, bathrooms, and bedding were all spotless, without the musty smell sometimes associated with older properties. In that respect, this hotel outperforms plenty of newer ones.

The mattress is on the firm side. Japanese hotel beds are generally firmer than those in China, so anyone used to a soft bed may need a night to adjust. Lighting is dim—not ideal if you want to read in the room—but the atmosphere is cozy and well suited to relaxing after a long day.

You may hear occasional traffic in rooms facing the Metropolitan Expressway, but with the windows shut it isn't much of an issue. If you're a light sleeper, ask the front desk for an inward-facing room at check-in.

The Tokyo Tower view is the hotel's greatest selling point. You don't have to fight crowds at an observation deck; you can stand by your window in slippers and take in the entire tower. When it lights up at night, it's a view that makes you want to book one more night.

---

## Service

Front-desk staff speak fluent English, which isn't a given at every midrange hotel in Japan. Luggage storage is free both before check-in and after checkout, a real convenience if you want to explore on your final day. Service overall is friendly and professional, consistent with Japan's strong hospitality culture.

---

## Breakfast

Breakfast is served at the ground-floor restaurant, and the selection is fairly small. Japanese options include miso soup, grilled fish, and rice; Western options include bread, eggs, and salad. Everything is fresh, but after a few consecutive mornings you may get bored.

Unless convenience is your top priority, I'd only book breakfast for one day and spend the other mornings discovering nearby cafés.

---

## Verdict

Tokyo Grand Hotel isn't the kind of property that dazzles you. Its style is restrained, dignified, and unmistakably from another era. But if you want a clean room, professional service, and Tokyo Tower right outside your window, it's one of the best options in its price range.

**Pros:** Quiet location near the action, tower views, excellent cleaning, and professional staff.  
**Cons:** Aging facilities, a basic breakfast, and dim lighting.

If you're comfortable with those trade-offs, you can book with confidence.
