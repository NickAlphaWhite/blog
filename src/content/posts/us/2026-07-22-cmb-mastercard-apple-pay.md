For years after Apple Pay arrived in China, UnionPay was the only card network you could add. This January, Visa finally broke the stalemate with eight banks at launch. Mastercard followed in April with four: Agricultural Bank of China, Bank of China, CITIC Bank, and Shanghai Pudong Development Bank.

Three months later, the second wave is here: **China Merchants Bank (CMB) and Ping An Bank** officially joined Mastercard on Apple Pay today, bringing the total number of participating banks to six.

CMB's addition deserves a story of its own. Its Mastercard debit card is currently one of the most accessible international payment cards in China for minors. Until now, you could only use the physical card. Finally, it can go in an iPhone Wallet.

This article has two parts: a quick overview of the latest launch and an in-depth review of what it's like to use CMB's Mastercard debit card.

## Mastercard on Apple Pay: Six Banks and Counting

Since April, Mastercard has been adding banks a little at a time.

**First wave (April 16, 2026):** Agricultural Bank of China, Bank of China, CITIC Bank, Shanghai Pudong Development Bank

**Second wave (today):** China Merchants Bank, Ping An Bank

**Still missing:** ICBC, China Construction Bank, Bank of Communications, Industrial Bank, HSBC, Bank of Shanghai, and others. Bank of Shanghai has confirmed that its newly launched Mastercard Fanfan credit card doesn't currently support Apple Pay.

Visa, by comparison, launched with eight banks in January 2026, with CCB, SPD Bank, Minsheng Bank, and Everbright Bank expected to join afterward. Mastercard's rollout has certainly been slower. But for specific customers, particularly minors using debit cards, Mastercard has an option Visa doesn't yet offer.

For now, Mastercard on Apple Pay is still limited to payments abroad. It doesn't work with domestic UnionPay point-of-sale terminals.

## Ping An Bank

Ping An's Mastercard lineup is mainly credit cards. With Apple Pay support, Ping An Mastercard credit card holders can pay overseas by tapping their iPhones, with transactions converted at Mastercard's exchange rate. If you have one, search for "Apple Pay" in the Ping An Bank app to link it.

Debit-card availability at Ping An is more limited. That's why most of this article focuses on China Merchants Bank: its Mastercard debit card is in a different league.

## An In-Depth Look at CMB's Mastercard Debit Card

This card didn't appear overnight. A quick timeline shows how CMB has positioned it.

- **Before June 2025:** Only a Gold version of the Mastercard debit card was offered, and it had to be opened as a Class I bank account. If you already had a Class I account at CMB, you needed to close or downgrade that account before opening this one. Transaction limits were subject to the bank's non-counter limits.
- **August 2025:** A standard version became available. It could be opened as a Class II account, so it no longer used up your one Class I account slot. That was the turning point: many people could finally use the Mastercard as a secondary card without giving up their main account.
- **2026:** Apple Pay support completed the picture.

The card is now available through CMB branches nationwide. Non-counter transaction limits vary depending on local anti-fraud banking policies. CMB's internal system assesses these limits automatically. In my case, I previously had an unrestricted Class I CMB account, but after downgrading my old card and opening the Mastercard, my new daily limit became RMB 50,000. That's worth thinking about in advance.

## Why I Think It's the Best: The Main Advantages

CMB's Mastercard debit card has several features that make it stand out.

### Automatic Settlement in Yuan—No Need to Buy Foreign Currency

This is the biggest difference between this card and a conventional dual-currency card. Purchases in foreign currencies are automatically settled in Chinese yuan using Mastercard's exchange rate. You don't have to buy foreign currency beforehand or keep a foreign-currency account. You spend yuan, and the card network handles the conversion at the prevailing rate.

That removes an entire currency-exchange step. It's especially valuable for minors, who often don't have foreign-currency accounts and may find it difficult to handle currency transactions at a bank counter. This card lets you skip the hassle.

### Cash Back on Every Purchase: 0.5%

Overseas purchases earn 0.5% cash back, paid as a subsidy. It isn't a spectacular rate, but the offer is straightforward: no minimum spending threshold, no stated cap, and automatic credits. It's a small extra discount on top of Mastercard's exchange rate.

The published promotion period ran through December 31, 2025. CMB has often renewed similar promotions, but the official bank announcement should be your source for current eligibility.

### Study-Abroad Transfers: 32% Off the Exchange-Rate Spread

If you use the account linked to this card to send money abroad for education, CMB's "Quick Remittance" service has offered a 32% discount on the exchange-rate spread. The spread can be one of the biggest hidden costs of sending money internationally, so the savings can be substantial on large transfers.

This offer was also published as valid through December 31, 2025.

### ATMs: No Bank Fee on the First Three Overseas Withdrawals

The first three overseas ATM withdrawals each month have been free of the bank's withdrawal fee. Other waived fees have included:

- Overseas ATM balance inquiries
- Currency conversion fees
- Overseas remittance fees (telecommunication charges still apply)

The standard version has slightly different ATM benefits: only the first overseas withdrawal each month is free, and overseas remittance fees aren't waived. The 0.5% cashback offer has been the same.

These fee waivers were listed as valid through December 31, 2025. Check the bank's official announcements for renewals.

### Real-Time Preauthorization Alerts

This is one of my favorite details in everyday use. When an overseas online payment triggers a preauthorization—for example, when linking your card to PayPal or buying something from Google Play—the CMB app immediately sends an alert showing the amount and merchant name.

Here's a practical benefit: when linking a card to PayPal, you may need to verify a four-digit code. Traditionally, you wait for the transaction to post before the code appears, which can take days. With CMB's real-time preauthorization information, you can see the code almost immediately, reducing the setup process from days to seconds.

### Finally, Apple Pay

With Apple Pay linked, most international contactless payment situations are covered. In Japan, you can tap your iPhone at convenience stores, drugstores, and compatible transit gates. In Singapore, you can tap on buses and the subway instead of buying a separate transit card. In Europe and North America, contactless terminals are widespread.

You don't enter the card number manually in Wallet. Open the card's details page in the China Merchants Bank app and select "Add to Apple Wallet." It opens Wallet with the card details filled in. After Face ID verification, setup takes just a few seconds.

## Compared With Other Mastercard Debit Cards

I'll skip a comparison table and get straight to the point.

In my view, CMB currently leads on three things: Apple Pay support, account access for minors, and automatic settlement in yuan at Mastercard's exchange rate.

CITIC Bank's Black Pixiu Mastercard debit card also supports Apple Pay, but opening it as a minor is more difficult. Some branches explicitly require a guardian and proof of sufficient deposits. Bank of China's Mastercard debit card still doesn't support Apple Pay and involves a more complicated account-opening process.

In short, CMB is the only one that checks all three boxes for me: I can open it, use Apple Pay, and have purchases settled automatically in yuan.

## Drawbacks and Limitations

A good card isn't necessarily a perfect card. Here are the issues I've encountered.

### Non-Counter Transaction Limits

This is the biggest drawback. CMB sets non-counter transaction limits automatically through its internal system, and you can't simply request a higher limit. After downgrading an old card and opening a new one, you might find that a previously unrestricted account now has a daily cap of RMB 50,000 or even less.

That limit directly affects Apple Pay purchases, online payments, and ATM withdrawals. For most everyday purchases, RMB 50,000 a day is enough. But for big expenses—such as airline tickets or tuition—you need to plan ahead, split payments over multiple days, or keep another card with a higher limit as a backup.

### Overseas Online Payments Must Be Enabled Manually

Online payments to overseas merchants aren't enabled by default. In the CMB app, go to "Me" → "Bank Cards" → "Quick Pay" → "Mastercard Cross-Border Online Payments" and turn the feature on. Once enabled, the default limit is $800 per day, also subject to the account's overall non-counter transaction limit.

It's an easy setting to miss. Plenty of people have had a first payment on a foreign website declined, only to discover a separate toggle hidden in the Quick Pay menu.

### Different Benefits on Standard and Gold Versions

The standard card introduced in August 2025 made the product much more accessible because it can be opened as a Class II account. But it offers fewer fee waivers than the Gold version. Only the first overseas ATM withdrawal per month is free, and overseas remittance fees aren't waived.

If you regularly withdraw cash abroad or send international transfers, the Gold version may save more. The trade-off is that it requires your Class I account slot.

### Limited Use in Mainland China

This card's real strength is overseas spending. Within mainland China, Apple Pay may use the UnionPay payment route, but not every point-of-sale terminal recognizes the card correctly. Some older devices only accept UnionPay card-number prefixes. If a payment fails, try another merchant rather than assuming the card is defective.

## Everyday Testing

### Linking to PayPal

It worked smoothly. Thanks to immediate preauthorization alerts, PayPal's four-digit verification code arrived right away, without waiting for the transaction to post. After linking, PayPal purchases worked normally using Mastercard's exchange rate.

### Google Play Store

Linking also worked. First, I had to enable cross-border online payments in Quick Pay. After that, in-app purchases and subscriptions charged successfully, at Mastercard's current exchange rate.

### In-Person Payments Abroad

I tested it in Japan and Singapore. Apple Pay worked at every Mastercard contactless terminal I tried: convenience stores, drugstores, subway gates, and restaurants. Acceptance was excellent.

The exchange rate was slightly better than UnionPay's in my tests, by around 0.2% to 0.5%. You won't necessarily notice that on one everyday purchase, but the difference can add up over time.

### Everyday Purchases in Mainland China

Where UnionPay contactless is accepted, you can use it for subway rides, convenience stores, and restaurants. Double-click to bring up Apple Pay, hold your iPhone to the NFC reader, and go. It's the same basic process as using a physical card, without reaching for your wallet.

## Who Is It For?

**Minors:** For young people who want access to international payments, I think this card is almost the only realistic option. Credit cards generally aren't available to applicants under 18. Many international debit cards have high requirements or aren't available to minors. CMB's Mastercard debit card plus Apple Pay is, in my experience, the most complete workable combination.

**Students studying abroad:** The remittance spread discount, overseas-purchase cash back, and waived ATM fees can add up to meaningful savings—particularly on large transfers, where a 32% discount on the exchange-rate spread can make a real difference.

**Frequent travelers:** If you travel internationally often, it works well as a primary way to pay. Between the convenience of Apple Pay and Mastercard's worldwide acceptance, you rarely need to take out the physical card.

## Final Thoughts

CMB's Mastercard debit card isn't perfect. It has non-counter transaction limits, a hidden setting for overseas online purchases, and differences in benefits between the Standard and Gold versions. But in my view, it's the strongest option in its category right now. For minors, the choice can feel less like "the best" and more like "the only one."

Apple Pay fills in the last missing piece. With a CMB Mastercard and an iPhone, you can pay in an enormous range of places worldwide without buying foreign currency in advance, without pulling out a physical card, and without waiting until you're 18.
